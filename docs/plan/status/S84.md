| S84-05 | S84 | ✅ | #2090 | **A plugin tracker write's audit entry waits for its caller's transaction** ([ADR 0211](../../adr/0211-a-trace-of-an-outside-write-waits-for-its-caller-s-transaction.md), accepted; finding #2031). `after_end` runs a callback once the session's transaction ends, commit or rollback; `wrote_in_transaction` says whether it executed a write or a flush; `session_scope`, `get_db`, `get_platform_db` and `in_scope` drain it after a rollback. The audit hook of `tracker/plugin` defers its entry to the end of the caller's transaction when that transaction already wrote, and writes at once otherwise, still in a transaction of its own. **Proves**: `apps/api/tests/test_plugin_tracker.py` (a `set_state` after the caller's UPDATE is audited once, in under 1 s, whether the caller commits or rolls back, and the caller's own write follows its transaction), `apps/api/tests/test_after_end.py` (after a commit and after a rollback in `session_scope`; in `get_db` and `get_platform_db` whether the route succeeds or fails; after a cancellation; a savepoint released or rolled back neither ends the wait nor clears `wrote_in_transaction`, even when its only write was rolled back; an `after_commit` queued by a released savepoint dies with the outer rollback; reads, an executed UPDATE, a flush, a commit and a rollback move `wrote_in_transaction` as they should). Removing the deferral (5 s, `database is locked`, no row), the drain after a rollback, the drain in `get_db`, the DML detection, the root-transaction guard, the cancellation drain, or draining `after_commit` callbacks on a rollback turns a test red. Two adversarial reviews found the savepoint cases and two cancellation windows (before the commit, and while a commit's callback awaits); all are fixed and tested. The second review also found that `after_commit`'s own listeners still treat a savepoint's end as the root's: older than this story, filed as finding #2089. **Does not prove**: `in_scope` (outposts) drains, by reading only, no test; a crash between the end of the transaction and the write (best effort, as `after_commit`); PostgreSQL, where nothing was lost before (the `python-postgres` CI job runs the suite there). |

| S84-07 | S84 | ✅ | — | **An `after_commit` callback follows the root transaction, not a savepoint's end** (finding #2089, ADR 0085). SQLAlchemy fires `after_commit` when a SAVEPOINT is released and `after_rollback` when one rolls back; the listeners now only record that outcome, and `after_transaction_end` decides: a released savepoint keeps what it registered for the root, a savepoint that rolls back drops only what it registered since it began (its mark, taken at `after_transaction_create`), the root's commit releases the list and its rollback or close drops it. **Proves**: `apps/api/tests/test_after_end.py` (a callback of a released savepoint does not run after the root rolls back and the session commits something else; a failed savepoint keeps the outer transaction's callbacks and drops its own; nested savepoints drop only the failed one's), red on the code before, and red again when the truncation is removed or the root's outcome ignored. **Does not prove**: a production path that met either case (an adversarial review of #2089 found none); PostgreSQL, run by the `python-postgres` CI job. |
